In 2022 the European Union registered "Ceylon Cinnamon" as a Protected Geographical Indication. For Sri Lanka that was a big moment, and for buyers it changed something real: in the EU, "Ceylon Cinnamon" is now a legally protected name rather than just a description.

Here's what that means in practice, without the legal fog.

## What a PGI actually is

A Protected Geographical Indication is a kind of intellectual property for products whose qualities or reputation are tied to a place. You already know plenty of them from cheese, ham and wine. For a PGI, at least one stage of production has to happen in the defined area. For Ceylon Cinnamon, the growing and processing both happen in Sri Lanka.

## What it protects

Three things, broadly:

- **The name itself.** Products that don't meet the registered specification can't be sold in the EU as Ceylon Cinnamon.
- **Against misuse and evocation.** Wording like "Ceylon-style", or imagery that implies the protected origin on a product that doesn't qualify, can fall foul of the rules.
- **A defined product.** The registered specification describes what Ceylon Cinnamon is, where it's made and how, including the characteristic layered quill of *Cinnamomum verum*.

## What it doesn't do

PGI isn't a grade, and it isn't a food safety certificate. A PGI product can still be C4 rather than Alba, and it still needs testing. You'll still want the SLS 81 grade (see our [grades guide](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide)) and a proper [Certificate of Analysis](/blog/certificate-of-analysis-cinnamon) for every lot.

## If you import or repack for the EU

The practical questions are about evidence:

- Can your supplier show Sri Lankan origin with a certificate of origin and lot traceability?
- Is the product, and the producer, covered by the PGI control arrangements before you put the name or the EU PGI symbol on your packs?
- Can you trace your own packs back to the export lot if anyone asks?

That last point is mostly about keeping good records. If your lot codes link back to the supplier's lot numbers, you're in good shape.

## If you're building a retail brand

PGI is one of the strongest origin messages you can have, because it's regulated. "Ceylon Cinnamon" with the right to use the PGI symbol carries far more weight than "premium" or "authentic". Just make sure you're actually entitled to it, keep it off any blend that contains cassia, and get the rest of the label right too.

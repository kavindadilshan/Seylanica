Cinnamon is grown outdoors, dried in open air and handled by people. It's a farm product, and like most farm products it carries some microbes. Because it's so dry, bacteria don't grow on it, but some of them can survive for a long time. That's fine if the cinnamon is going into a cake that gets baked. It's a real issue if it's dusted onto a latte or stirred into a chilled dessert.

That's why microbiology sits near the top of every powder buyer's checklist.

## The usual panel

| Test | What it tells you | Typical expectation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total plate count (TPC) | Overall microbial load; a hygiene indicator | Set by buyer, lower for ready-to-eat uses |
| Yeasts and moulds | Drying and storage conditions | Set by buyer |
| *E. coli* | Faecal contamination, hygiene | Low or not detected |
| *Salmonella* | Pathogen | Absent in 25 g |

Industry bodies like the European Spice Association and the American Spice Trade Association publish guidance values that many buyers use as a starting point. Your limits should reflect what you're making and whether there's a kill step after the cinnamon goes in.

*Salmonella* is the one regulators in both the EU and US watch most closely in spices. "Absent in 25 g" is the standard expectation almost everywhere.

## Treated or untreated?

**Untreated** powder can be acceptable for products that go through a validated cooking step, depending on the buyer.

**Steam-sterilised** powder has its microbial load cut significantly with saturated steam, with no irradiation and no chemical residues. Done well, the effect on aroma is small.

## How good suppliers keep counts down

Testing finds problems; processing prevents them. The basics:

- dry the bark off the ground and under cover;
- keep processing areas clean, with pest control and food-grade packaging;
- control moisture so nothing can grow in storage (see [moisture in cinnamon](/blog/cinnamon-moisture-content-iso-939));
- keep soil out, because soil carries microbes (high acid-insoluble ash is often an early warning; see [ash tests](/blog/cinnamon-ash-acid-insoluble-ash));
- test every milled batch and only release lots that pass.

## Writing it into your spec

List each organism with its limit and test method (ISO methods are widely used). State whether you need steam-sterilised or untreated product. And insist on positive release: no lot ships until results are in and within spec, especially for *Salmonella*.

The first time most buyers see a Ceylon cinnamon price list, it reads like a code: Alba, C5 Sp, C5, C4, M5, H1. It's simpler than it looks. Nearly all of it comes down to one measurement, the diameter of the quill, plus a few checks on colour and condition.

## How a quill gets its grade

Grading follows the Sri Lanka Standard SLS 81. Graders look at:

- **Diameter.** This is the big one. Thinner quills get higher grades.
- **Quills per kilogram**, which tracks thickness and is a handy consistency check.
- **Colour and foxing.** Foxing is reddish-brown patching on the bark. Finer grades allow less.
- **Length and completeness.** Standard quills are about 42 cm.
- **Broken pieces and foreign matter.**

The naming has a pattern worth memorising: the letter tells you the family, and in the Continental and Mexican families **a higher number means a thinner quill**. C5 is finer than C4. (Hamburg grades run the other way, just to keep everyone on their toes; H1 is the finest of the three.) The standard itself is covered in [our SLS 81 explainer](/blog/sls-81-standard-explained).

## The four families

| Family | Grades | Relative thickness | What it's like | Who usually buys it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alba | Alba | Thinnest, 6 mm or less | Most delicate and sweet | Luxury retail, gifting, fine dining |
| Continental | C5 Special, C5, C4, C3 | Thin to medium | Balanced, sweet and woody | Importers, gourmet retail, manufacturers |
| Mexican | M5, M4 | Medium-thick | Fuller | Latin American markets, infusion |
| Hamburg | H1, H2, H3 | Thickest | Bold and woody | Grinding, extraction |

For reference, our own typical diameters are 6 mm or less for Alba, about 8 to 10 mm for C5 Special, about 10 to 12 mm for C5 and about 13 to 14 mm for C4. The binding figures for your order are always on the spec sheet and the lot COA.

## Alba

Pencil-thin, pale gold, and the hardest grade to make. Alba is what you buy when the stick itself is the product: a glass tube on a deli shelf, a gift box, a stick served with coffee in a good hotel. We've given it [its own article](/blog/alba-cinnamon-grade).

## The Continental grades

This is where most of the trade happens.

**C5 Special** is the premium end, with very even colour and a tight roll. Gourmet retail and spice merchants like it. **C5** is the all-rounder that importers and repackers buy in volume. **C4** is thicker and better value, and it's excellent in food service and anything simmered. **C3** is thicker again and often ends up milled.

If you're choosing between the first two, read [C5 Special vs C5](/blog/c5-special-vs-c5-cinnamon). If cost is the priority, [C4](/blog/c4-cinnamon-grade-uses) deserves a serious look.

## Mexican grades

Mexico has long been one of the biggest buyers of Ceylon cinnamon, and its market liked a sturdier, medium-thick quill for drinks and desserts. The trade named those grades after it. More in [Mexican grades M5 and M4](/blog/mexican-grade-cinnamon-m5-m4).

## Hamburg grades

The thickest quills, with more foxing allowed. They're not pretty, and they don't need to be. Their job is to deliver Ceylon flavour into a grinder or an extraction vessel at the lowest cost per kilo of any whole quill. See [Hamburg grades](/blog/hamburg-grade-cinnamon).

## Below the quills

Not everything becomes a quill. Broken pieces become **quillings**, thin shavings become **featherings**, and trimmings become **chips**. All of it is genuine Ceylon bark, and it's what most powder, tea-cut and bark oil is made from. We explain each in [quillings, featherings and chips](/blog/quillings-featherings-chips).

## How we'd choose

Ask yourself four questions.

1. **Will your customer see the stick?** If yes, look at Alba or C5 Special. If it disappears into food, look at C4, Hamburg or by-grades.
2. **How delicate is the product?** Light custards and tea suit finer grades. Curries, braises and mulled drinks are happy with thicker ones.
3. **What's your price point?** Work back from your retail price or recipe cost.
4. **What do your samples say?** Put two neighbouring grades side by side in your product and decide with your eyes and your tongue.

The thing we'd most want a new buyer to understand is that grade measures thickness and finish. It doesn't measure authenticity. Every grade is true Ceylon cinnamon.

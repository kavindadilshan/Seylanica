Hold an Alba quill up to the light and you'll understand the price. It's barely thicker than a pencil, pale gold from end to end, and so evenly rolled that it looks like one piece of bark even though it's made of many. It's the grade Sri Lankan peelers are proudest of, and the one buyers most often over-order.

## What makes a quill Alba

- **Diameter of 6 mm or less**, the thinnest of all SLS 81 grades.
- **Pale, even golden colour** with very little foxing.
- **A tight, uniform roll** built from many fine layers.
- **Standard 42 cm length**, often cut to 5, 8 or 10 cm for retail.

Our own Alba specification adds moisture of 12% or less (ISO 939), volatile oil of at least 1.0% v/w typical (ISO 6571) and foreign matter of 1% or less (ISO 927). The full sheet is in our [catalog](/products?spec=quills-alba).

## Why it costs what it does

Making Alba needs the youngest, thinnest shoots and the steadiest hands. Strips have to be cut finer and overlapped more precisely than for any other grade, and only experienced peelers can do it consistently. Even then, only a small part of each harvest comes out at Alba size. Every quill is checked for diameter and colour before it's packed.

Scarcity plus skill equals price. We explain the wider picture in [why Ceylon cinnamon costs more](/blog/why-ceylon-cinnamon-costs-more).

## How it tastes

Alba is Ceylon cinnamon at its most refined. Sweet, floral and citrus-bright, with a soft warmth that lingers and no harshness at all. It's at its best where other flavours are light too: poached fruit, milk puddings, delicate pastry, a good cup of coffee.

## Where it's worth the money

- **Luxury and gourmet retail**, where the sticks sit in a clear tube or jar and sell on looks.
- **Gift boxes and hampers.** There are some ideas in [cinnamon gift boxes](/blog/cinnamon-gift-boxes-hampers).
- **Hotels and fine dining**, where a stick is served with a drink or dessert.
- **Speciality tea and coffee**, when the stick is part of the presentation.

## Where it usually isn't

If the cinnamon is going to be ground, steeped and strained, or simmered in a curry, nobody will ever see how beautiful it was. For those jobs we'd point you to [C5 Special or C5](/blog/c5-special-vs-c5-cinnamon), [C4](/blog/c4-cinnamon-grade-uses) or [chips](/blog/quillings-featherings-chips). You'll keep the Ceylon flavour and save a lot of money.

## Checking you've got real Alba

Because Alba commands a premium, it's worth verifying:

- **Measure.** Take a random handful of quills from different cartons and check the diameters with calipers.
- **Count.** Quills per kilo is a quick sanity check on thickness.
- **Look.** Colour should be pale and even, with minimal foxing.
- **Read the lab report.** Moisture, volatile oil and foreign matter should match the spec.
- **Match the lot.** The sample you approved and the goods you received should carry the same lot number.

## Pack it carefully

**Importing Ceylon cinnamon into the United States requires an FDA-registered supplier facility, Prior Notice for each shipment, supplier verification under FSVP, and customs entry with correct classification and labelling.** This guide outlines each step. Requirements change, so confirm details with the FDA and a licensed customs broker.

## 1. FDA food facility registration

Foreign facilities that manufacture, process, pack or hold food for US consumption must register with the FDA and renew registration every two years, and they must designate a **US agent**. Ask your supplier for their registration status before ordering.

## 2. Prior Notice

The FDA must receive **Prior Notice** of each food shipment before it arrives, within set time windows depending on mode of transport. It is filed electronically, often by the customs broker, and the confirmation number accompanies the shipment.

## 3. Foreign Supplier Verification Program (FSVP)

Under the Food Safety Modernization Act (FSMA), most importers must:

- Conduct a **hazard analysis** for each food.
- **Evaluate the supplier's** performance and the risk posed by the food.
- Conduct **supplier verification activities**, such as reviewing COAs, sampling and testing, or audits.
- Take **corrective actions** when needed.
- Keep **records**.

For cinnamon, key hazards include *Salmonella* in ground product and chemical contaminants. See [microbiological testing](/blog/microbiology-testing-spices) and [heavy metals](/blog/heavy-metals-spices-testing).

## 4. Food safety expectations

- **Pathogens:** the FDA treats *Salmonella* in spices as a known risk; many importers require treated powder or a validated kill step.
- **Pesticides:** residues must be within EPA tolerances; residues without a tolerance can make food adulterated. See [pesticide residues](/blog/pesticide-residues-eu-mrl-cinnamon).
- **Treatments:** irradiation is permitted for spices with labelling; EtO is registered with tolerances in the US. See [steam vs irradiation vs EtO](/blog/steam-sterilisation-vs-irradiation-spices).
- **Contaminants:** lead and other elements are an FDA focus area for spices.

## 5. Customs entry

- **Classification** under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS), in heading 0906. See [HS codes for cinnamon](/blog/hs-codes-cinnamon).
- **Importer Security Filing (ISF)** for ocean shipments before loading.
- **Customs bond** and entry filing by a licensed broker.
- **Duties and any additional tariffs** in force at the time of entry; check current rates.

## 6. Labelling

Retail packs must follow FDA labelling rules: statement of identity, net quantity (US customary and metric), name and place of business, ingredient list where applicable, country of origin marking, and allergen declarations if relevant. See [labelling cinnamon for the EU, UK and US](/blog/cinnamon-label-requirements-eu-us).

## 7. Documents per shipment

Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, COA, and Prior Notice confirmation. See [the export documents checklist](/blog/export-documents-ceylon-cinnamon).

## Summary

US import compliance centres on the FDA: registered suppliers, Prior Notice and FSVP verification, backed by lot data. Build FSVP records from your supplier's COAs and your own spot tests.

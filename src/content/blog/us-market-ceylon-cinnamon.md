**In the US, most everyday cinnamon is cassia, so Ceylon cinnamon stands out as a premium, origin-specific alternative.** Growth has come through specialty retail, e-commerce and brands that explain the difference clearly. Success requires careful FDA compliance and factual marketing.

## The opportunity

- **Clear differentiation:** Ceylon tastes and looks different from the cassia most Americans know. See [Ceylon vs cassia](/blog/ceylon-vs-cassia-cinnamon-differences).
- **Premium and specialty retail** looking for unique products.
- **E-commerce** making niche products accessible nationally. See [selling on Amazon](/blog/selling-cinnamon-on-amazon).
- **Food service** interest in quality spices. See [food service supply](/blog/food-service-cinnamon-supply).

## Compliance essentials

- **FDA facility registration** of the supplier.
- **Prior Notice** for each shipment.
- **FSVP** supplier verification by the importer.
- **Pathogen control:** treated powder or validated kill steps.
- **Residues** within EPA tolerances.
- **Labelling** to FDA rules, including country of origin.

See [importing into the USA](/blog/importing-cinnamon-into-usa-fda).

## Positioning tips

- Lead with **species and origin**: "True cinnamon from Sri Lanka."
- Explain **flavour**: sweet and floral versus hot and pungent.
- Show **the layered quill**. See [product photography](/blog/cinnamon-product-photography).
- **Avoid health claims**, which are common in online cinnamon marketing but risky. See [marketing without health claims](/blog/compliant-marketing-claims-spices).

## Pricing

Ceylon's premium must be justified by clear value. Offer good-better-best ranges by grade and pack size. See [retail pricing](/blog/retail-pricing-cinnamon).

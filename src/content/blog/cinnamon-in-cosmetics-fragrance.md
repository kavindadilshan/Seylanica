**Cinnamon brings warm, spicy notes to fragrances, candles and personal care, but its key aroma compounds are known skin sensitisers, so use is tightly regulated.** Formulators need to understand which cinnamon material they are using, its composition, and the allergen and usage rules that apply.

## Cinnamon materials

| Material | Main constituents | Character | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bark oil | Cinnamaldehyde (cinnamal) | Classic sweet cinnamon | Fine fragrance (low levels), flavour |
| Leaf oil | Eugenol | Clove-like, spicy | Fragrance, candles, household |
| Extracts and oleoresins | Varies | Rich, rounded | Specialised uses |
| Ground bark | Whole spice | Natural texture | Some scrubs, soaps (with care) |

See [bark oil vs leaf oil](/blog/cinnamon-bark-oil-vs-leaf-oil).

## Safety and regulation

### Skin sensitisation

Cinnamal, cinnamyl alcohol and eugenol can cause allergic skin reactions in sensitive people. This drives the rules below.

### EU allergen labelling

Under the EU Cosmetics Regulation, listed fragrance allergens, including cinnamal, cinnamyl alcohol and eugenol, must be declared in the ingredients list when present above set thresholds (historically 0.001% in leave-on and 0.01% in rinse-off products). The list of labelled allergens has been expanded in recent rule changes, so check the current requirements and transition dates.

### IFRA standards

The International Fragrance Association (IFRA) sets standards that restrict or limit the use of certain materials, including cinnamal and cinnamon bark oil, by product category. Members and many brands follow these as a condition of supply.

### Safety assessment

Cosmetic products in the EU and UK require a safety assessment by a qualified person. Other markets have their own requirements.

## Documentation to request

- Botanical source (*Cinnamomum verum*) and plant part.
- Composition (GC profile) and allergen statement.
- IFRA conformity certificate where relevant.
- Safety Data Sheet (SDS).
- Certificate of origin.

## Marketing caution

Avoid therapeutic or medicinal claims in cosmetics marketing; they can reclassify a product and breach rules. Factual statements about scent and origin are safer. See [marketing without health claims](/blog/compliant-marketing-claims-spices).

## Candles and home fragrance

Cinnamon is a seasonal favourite in candles and diffusers. These products have their own safety and labelling rules (for example, CLP hazard labelling in the EU for mixtures containing sensitisers).

## Summary

Cinnamon is a powerful note with real safety obligations. Know your material, follow allergen labelling and IFRA limits, and keep documentation complete.

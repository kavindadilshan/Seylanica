You can see grit on a quill. You can't see it in a spoonful of powder. That's why two slightly unglamorous lines on the lab report, total ash and acid-insoluble ash, do so much work for powder buyers.

## Total ash (ISO 928)

The lab weighs a sample and burns it at high temperature until only the inorganic residue is left. That residue includes the minerals that are naturally in cinnamon bark, things like calcium and potassium, plus any mineral dirt that came along for the ride.

So some total ash is completely normal. It's when it climbs above the usual range that you start asking questions.

## Acid-insoluble ash (ISO 930)

This is the more telling test. The lab takes the ash and treats it with hydrochloric acid. The plant's own minerals dissolve. Sand (silica) and soil mostly don't. What's left is the acid-insoluble ash, and it's a pretty direct measure of:

- soil that got onto the bark during harvesting or drying;
- cleaning and sorting that weren't thorough enough;
- poor storage.

## What limits look like

| Parameter | Our powder specification |
| --- | --- |
| Total ash | 5% m/m or less (ISO 928) |
| Acid-insoluble ash | 1% m/m or less (ISO 930) |

Industry guidance, such as the reference values published by the European Spice Association, is a common starting point for buyers setting their own limits.

## Why you should care beyond the number

**You're paying for dirt.** Sand in cinnamon is sold at the price of cinnamon.

**Sand is hard on equipment.** Grinders, sieves and dosing systems wear faster.

**Customers notice grit**, especially in smooth products like icings and drinks.

**It's a hygiene signal.** Soil carries microbes, so lots with high acid-insoluble ash often have higher microbial counts too. If one number is up, check the other. Our article on [microbiological testing](/blog/microbiology-testing-spices) explains that panel.

## How good processors keep ash down

Most of it is basic good practice. Dry quills on raised racks rather than on the ground. Work in clean, covered areas. Sieve, aspirate and use magnets on chips and by-grades. Sort out discoloured or dirty pieces before milling. We describe the steps in [drying and processing cinnamon](/blog/cinnamon-drying-processing).

## In your spec

Put both lines in, with methods and limits, and ask for them on every powder COA. For quills and chips, add foreign matter (ISO 927) as well. The spec template in [how to read a spec sheet](/blog/how-to-read-cinnamon-spec-sheet) shows where they sit.

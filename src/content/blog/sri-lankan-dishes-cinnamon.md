**In Sri Lanka, cinnamon is as much a savoury spice as a sweet one.** It goes into curry powders, rice, meat and vegetable curries and festive sweets. For cooks and food brands, Sri Lankan cuisine is the best showcase of Ceylon cinnamon's range.

## How cinnamon is used at home

- **Tempering:** pieces of quill fried briefly in oil with curry leaves, onions and other whole spices, releasing aroma at the start of cooking.
- **Simmering:** whole pieces in curries and rice dishes, removed or left in.
- **Curry powders:** ground cinnamon in spice blends, both raw and roasted.
- **Sweets:** ground cinnamon in festive and everyday sweets.

## Signature uses

### Roasted curry powder

Dry-roasted whole spices, often including coriander, cumin, fennel, cinnamon, cardamom, cloves and curry leaves, ground into a dark, aromatic blend used for meat curries. See [cinnamon in spice blends](/blog/cinnamon-spice-blends).

### Yellow rice (kaha bath)

Rice cooked with turmeric, coconut milk, cardamom, cloves, cinnamon and pandan or curry leaves, often served at celebrations.

### Meat and fish curries

Chicken, beef and fish curries commonly include cinnamon pieces in the tempering and roasted curry powder in the base.

### Vegetable curries

Cinnamon adds warmth to dishes made with jackfruit, beetroot, pumpkin and more.

### Sweets

Cinnamon features in many traditional sweets, adding a fragrant note alongside jaggery, coconut and cardamom.

## Tips for cooks outside Sri Lanka

- Use **C4 or C5 quills** broken into pieces for tempering and simmering. See [C4 cinnamon](/blog/c4-cinnamon-grade-uses).
- Use **fresh powder** in curry blends; roast whole spices yourself for depth.
- **Balance:** cinnamon should support, not dominate, savoury dishes.
- Pair with **curry leaves, pandan, cardamom and coconut** for authentic flavour.

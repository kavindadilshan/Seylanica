**Ceylon cinnamon prices move with harvests, labour, demand, freight and currency, and without a futures market, buyers must manage that risk through contracts, inventory and pricing strategy.** Done well, volatility becomes manageable rather than a margin shock.

## Why prices move

| Driver | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Harvest volume and weather | Supply shortfalls raise prices. See [climate and supply](/blog/climate-impact-cinnamon-supply) |
| Labour availability | Peeler shortages raise costs. See [skills gap](/blog/cinnamon-skills-training-peelers) |
| Global demand | Strong demand pulls prices up |
| Exchange rates | Currency moves change export prices |
| Freight costs | Shipping rate swings affect landed cost |
| Regulation and testing | Extra requirements add cost |

See [what determines the price of Ceylon cinnamon](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-price-factors).

## Where volatility hurts

- Brands with **fixed retail prices** agreed months ahead.
- Manufacturers with **long-term customer contracts**.
- Importers holding **unhedged stock** in a falling market.

## Tools to manage risk

### Supply contracts

Lock in prices for defined periods or volumes. See [annual contracts vs spot](/blog/annual-supply-contracts-cinnamon).

### Forward buying

Buy ahead when prices are favourable, balancing storage costs and freshness. See [shelf life](/blog/cinnamon-shelf-life-storage).

### Safety stock

Buffer stock gives time to react to price spikes. See [planning around lead times](/blog/lead-times-planning-cinnamon).

### Grade flexibility

Approve adjacent grades or by-grades where acceptable, to switch when one grade spikes. See [grades explained](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide).

### Customer pricing clauses

Build cost-review clauses into customer contracts, linked to defined indicators or thresholds.

### Currency management

Agree contract currency carefully, and consider currency hedging through your bank for large exposures.

### Supplier relationships

Suppliers share market intelligence with long-term partners: harvest outlook, availability and price direction.

## A simple risk policy

1. Contract **60 to 80%** of forecast volume.
2. Hold **safety stock** for key grades.
3. **Review prices** quarterly.
4. **Qualify alternatives** in advance.
5. **Track** origin prices and freight.

## Summary

You cannot remove volatility, but you can plan for it. Combine contracts, stock, flexibility and good information, and pass a fair share of cost movements through to customers.

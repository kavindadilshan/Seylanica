If you write "C5 quills" in a purchase order, what exactly have you asked for? On its own, the phrase is a bit loose. With "according to SLS 81" after it, it becomes something both sides can measure. That's the whole point of the standard.

## What SLS 81 is

SLS 81 is the Sri Lanka Standard specification for cinnamon, published by the Sri Lanka Standards Institution. It's the reference the industry uses to define what each grade means, and it has been revised over the years. Our own spec sheets currently cite SLS 81:2021.

It covers more than whole quills. The standard also deals with quillings, featherings, chips and ground cinnamon, and sets out:

- **Grade definitions**, grouped into the Alba, Continental, Mexican and Hamburg families.
- **Physical limits** for each grade: maximum diameter, minimum number of whole quills per kilo, how much foxing is allowed, length and completeness.
- **Quality requirements** such as moisture, ash, volatile oil and extraneous matter.
- **How to sample and test**, often by pointing to ISO methods.

## Why "limits" matters

A grade isn't a single number. It's a set of boundaries. A C5 quill has to stay under a maximum diameter, meet a minimum count per kilo and stay within the allowed foxing. A quill that's too thick for C5 drops to C4, and so on down.

That's what makes grading auditable. A buyer can take a sample, measure it with calipers, count quills per kilo and see whether the shipment matches what was ordered. If you want a plain-language tour of the grades themselves, start with [Ceylon cinnamon grades explained](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide).

## How it fits with ISO standards

In practice, contracts lean on SLS 81 for grade and ISO standards for test methods. The ones you'll see most:

| Standard | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| SLS 81 | Ceylon cinnamon grades and quality requirements |
| ISO 6539 | International specification for Sri Lankan type cinnamon |
| ISO 939 | Moisture, by distillation |
| ISO 6571 | Volatile oil |
| ISO 927 | Extraneous and foreign matter |
| ISO 928 / ISO 930 | Total ash / acid-insoluble ash |

If a spec sheet quotes numbers with no method beside them, ask which method was used. Two labs using different methods can get different answers. Our guide to [reading a spec sheet](/blog/how-to-read-cinnamon-spec-sheet) walks through each line.

## What SLS 81 won't tell you

A perfect C5 quill can still fail your market's food safety rules. Grade says nothing about:

- microbes, especially in powder ([microbiological testing](/blog/microbiology-testing-spices));
- pesticide residues against your market's MRLs;
- heavy metals;
- coumarin, if your product has limits.

Those come from the lab report for the lot, not the grade.

## Putting it in a contract

Here's wording we'd be happy to see from a buyer:

> Ceylon cinnamon quills, *Cinnamomum verum*, grade C5 according to SLS 81:2021, length 42 cm, moisture max 12% (ISO 939), volatile oil min 0.8% v/w (ISO 6571), country of origin Sri Lanka. Each lot supplied with a Certificate of Analysis.

It's short, it's testable, and it leaves very little room for argument later. For a complete template, see [writing a purchase specification](/blog/writing-cinnamon-purchase-specification).

Disputes about "is this really C5?" mostly disappear once both sides agree on SLS 81 for grade and ISO methods for the numbers. Every [spec sheet in our catalog](/products) is written that way.

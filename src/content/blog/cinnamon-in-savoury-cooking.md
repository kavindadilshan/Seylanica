**Cinnamon is a classic savoury spice in many of the world's cuisines, adding warmth and depth to curries, braises, rice and sauces.** Ceylon cinnamon is particularly well suited to savoury food because its delicate profile supports other flavours rather than dominating them.

## Savoury traditions

| Cuisine | Dishes | How cinnamon is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sri Lankan | Meat curries, yellow rice | Tempering, roasted curry powder. See [Sri Lankan cooking](/blog/sri-lankan-dishes-cinnamon) |
| Indian | Biryani, korma, garam masala | Whole in rice, ground in blends |
| Moroccan | Tagines, pastilla | Ground, with ginger and saffron |
| Middle Eastern | Rice pilafs, kofta, stuffed vegetables | Ground, in baharat |
| Greek | Moussaka, pastitsio, stifado | Ground in meat sauces, whole in stews |
| Mexican | Mole, braises | Whole sticks, toasted and ground |
| Vietnamese | Pho broth | Whole bark, toasted |
| Persian | Stews and rice | Ground, with turmeric |

## How to use it

- **Whole pieces** in stocks, braises, rice and curries; remove before serving.
- **Toasted** whole cinnamon for deeper notes in broths and moles.
- **Ground** in rubs, marinades and spice pastes.
- **Finishing:** a light dusting on some rice and meat dishes.

## Pairing guide

- **Meats:** lamb, beef, chicken, duck.
- **Vegetables:** squash, aubergine, sweet potato, tomato, carrot.
- **Spices:** cumin, coriander, cardamom, clove, allspice, ginger, chilli.
- **Aromatics:** onion, garlic, bay, curry leaf.
- **Fruit:** dried apricots, prunes, raisins in tagines and pilafs.

## Dosage

Start small. Cinnamon should give depth and roundness; if the dish tastes "sweet", reduce it. Ceylon's gentler profile is forgiving, which is why many chefs prefer it in savoury cooking.

**Ceylon cinnamon is sweeter, more floral and gentler than cassia, so it transforms bakes that need elegance rather than heat.** When swapping from cassia, most bakers start with the same amount and adjust upwards, often to around 1.25 to 1.5 times, to reach the intensity they want.

## How Ceylon behaves in baking

| | Ceylon | Cassia |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flavour | Sweet, floral, citrus, gentle warmth | Strong, hot, slightly bitter |
| Best in | Custards, pastries, cakes, buns, dairy desserts | Bold spice cakes, mass-market bakes |
| Dosage | Same or higher | Lower |
| Colour | Lighter tan | Darker |

See [Ceylon vs cassia](/blog/ceylon-vs-cassia-cinnamon-differences).

## Bakes where Ceylon shines

- **Cinnamon buns and rolls:** layered, aromatic fillings without bitterness.
- **Custards, crème brûlée and rice pudding:** infuse with a stick for floral depth.
- **Apple pies and crumbles:** complements fruit without overpowering.
- **Shortbread and butter biscuits:** delicate spice against butter.
- **Cakes and muffins:** rounded warmth.
- **Pastry creams and frostings:** fine powder dissolves smoothly.

## Substitution guide

1. **Start equal:** replace cassia 1:1.
2. **Taste the raw batter or filling** (where safe) or bake a test batch.
3. **Adjust:** increase by 25 to 50% if you want more intensity.
4. **Balance:** pair with a little nutmeg, cardamom or vanilla to build depth.

## Format tips

- **Powder:** use 60 mesh for doughs and batters, 80 mesh for frostings and smooth fillings. See [powder mesh sizes](/blog/cinnamon-powder-mesh-size).
- **Sticks:** infuse milk or cream for custards and sauces, then remove.
- **Freshness:** store powder sealed and use within its best-before for full aroma. See [shelf life and storage](/blog/cinnamon-shelf-life-storage).

## For commercial bakeries

- **Consistency:** specify volatile oil and mesh, and approve against a reference sample. See [volatile oil](/blog/volatile-oil-cinnamon-iso-6571).
- **Coumarin:** EU maximum levels apply to coumarin in certain bakery wares, such as 50 mg/kg for traditional and/or seasonal bakery with cinnamon in the labelling and 15 mg/kg for other fine bakery ware. Ceylon's naturally low coumarin gives formulators headroom. See [coumarin explained](/blog/coumarin-in-cinnamon-explained).
- **Microbiology:** if cinnamon is added after baking, as in toppings or fillings, use treated powder. See [microbiological testing](/blog/microbiology-testing-spices).
- **Labelling:** "made with Ceylon cinnamon" is a strong selling point when supply supports it.

## A simple test recipe: cinnamon sugar

Mix 1 part Ceylon cinnamon powder with 4 parts caster sugar. Taste side by side with a cassia version on buttered toast. It is the fastest way to show your team the difference.

Ceylon cinnamon brings sweetness and elegance to bakes. Swap 1:1, taste and adjust, and choose the right mesh. For bakeries, its low natural coumarin is a practical advantage too. [Request powder samples](/quote?type=sample) for a side-by-side bake test.

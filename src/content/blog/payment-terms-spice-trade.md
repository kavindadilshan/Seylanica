**Bulk Ceylon cinnamon is usually paid for by bank transfer (T/T) with a deposit and the balance against shipping documents, or by a letter of credit (L/C) at sight.** Each option balances risk differently between buyer and seller. Choosing well, and protecting the payment process, is part of good sourcing.

## The main options

| Term | How it works | Buyer risk | Seller risk | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T/T, full advance | Buyer pays before production | High | Low | Low |
| T/T, deposit + balance against documents | Deposit (often 30 to 50%), balance on copy B/L | Medium | Medium | Low |
| Documentary collection (D/P) | Banks release documents against payment | Lower | Higher | Medium |
| L/C at sight | Buyer's bank guarantees payment against compliant documents | Low | Low | Higher |
| Open account | Buyer pays after receipt | Low | High | Low |

## T/T with deposit

The most common term for established relationships:

1. Buyer pays the **deposit** on order confirmation.
2. Exporter prepares, tests and ships the lot.
3. Exporter sends **copies** of shipping documents.
4. Buyer pays the **balance**; exporter releases original documents or telex-releases the B/L.

Simple and cheap, but it requires trust. Start with a smaller trial order to build it.

## Letter of credit

An L/C is a bank's promise to pay the seller if the seller presents documents that comply exactly with the credit's terms.

**Tips:**

- Share the **draft L/C** with the supplier before issuing.
- Keep **document requirements** realistic and consistent with the contract.
- Allow enough **time** for shipment and presentation.
- Expect **bank fees** on both sides.

Discrepancies, even typos, can delay payment. See [the export documents checklist](/blog/export-documents-ceylon-cinnamon).

## Documentary collection

The seller's bank sends documents to the buyer's bank, which releases them only when the buyer pays (D/P) or accepts a bill of exchange (D/A). Cheaper than an L/C, but the seller has no bank guarantee of payment.

## Protecting against payment fraud

Business email compromise is a real risk in international trade. Fraudsters intercept emails and send "updated" bank details.

- **Verify bank details** by phone or another trusted channel, never only by email.
- Be suspicious of **last-minute changes** to beneficiary accounts.
- Make sure the **beneficiary name** matches the supplier's legal name.

See [avoiding fraud when sourcing spices](/blog/avoiding-spice-sourcing-scams).

## Summary

T/T with deposit is efficient once trust exists; an L/C offers security for new relationships at higher cost. Whatever you choose, verify bank details independently.

The cinnamon trade has its own vocabulary, and most of it never gets explained. New buyers pick it up by nodding along on calls and looking things up afterwards. We'd rather just write it down. This is the glossary we send to people who are new to sourcing Ceylon cinnamon, grouped by where you'll run into each term.

## On the product itself

**Alba.** The thinnest, finest grade of quill, 6 mm or less across. The most expensive grade. We wrote a whole piece on [Alba](/blog/alba-cinnamon-grade).

**Bark oil.** Essential oil distilled from cinnamon bark, dominated by cinnamaldehyde. Smells like classic cinnamon. Not to be confused with leaf oil.

**Chips.** Bark trimmings and bark too coarse or thin to roll into quills. Used for distillation and grinding.

**Continental grades.** The "C" grades: C5 Special, C5, C4 and C3. The workhorses of the export trade.

**Cut sticks.** Quills cut down to retail lengths, usually 5, 8 or 10 cm.

**Featherings.** Thin inner shavings and small pieces of bark from peeling.

**Foxing.** Reddish-brown patches on the bark surface. Finer grades allow less of it.

**Hamburg grades.** H1, H2 and H3, the thickest quills, mainly for grinding. Unusually, H1 is the finest of the three.

**Leaf oil.** Oil distilled from cinnamon leaves, dominated by eugenol. Smells more like clove than cinnamon. The differences are in [bark oil vs leaf oil](/blog/cinnamon-bark-oil-vs-leaf-oil).

**Mexican grades.** M5 and M4, medium-thick quills historically popular in Mexico.

**Quill.** A long roll of overlapping inner-bark strips, traditionally 42 cm.

**Quillings.** Broken pieces of quill.

**SLS 81.** The Sri Lanka Standard that defines cinnamon grades. Covered in [SLS 81 explained](/blog/sls-81-standard-explained).

**Tea cut.** Small, sieved pieces, often 3 to 5 mm, for tea bags and blends.

## On the lab report

**Acid-insoluble ash (AIA).** The bit of ash that won't dissolve in acid, which is mostly sand and soil. A cleanliness check (ISO 930).

**COA.** Certificate of Analysis, the lab report for one specific lot. What a good one looks like is in [this guide](/blog/certificate-of-analysis-cinnamon).

**Coumarin.** A natural flavour compound. Very low in Ceylon, much higher in cassia. Measured by HPLC.

**Foreign matter.** Anything that isn't cinnamon bark: stems, leaves, stones (ISO 927).

**HPLC.** High-performance liquid chromatography, the usual method for measuring coumarin.

**Mesh.** How fine a powder is. 80 mesh is finer than 60 mesh.

**Moisture.** Water content, tested by ISO 939. Too much and you risk mould.

**MRL.** Maximum residue level, the legal limit for a pesticide residue in a given market.

**Steam sterilisation.** A heat treatment that reduces microbes without irradiation or chemicals.

**TPC.** Total plate count, a general measure of how many microbes are in a powder.

**Volatile oil.** The aromatic oil content, tested by ISO 6571. Broadly, more oil means more aroma.

## On the quote and the shipping papers

**B/L.** Bill of lading, the shipping line's receipt and title document for sea freight.

**CIF, CFR, FOB, DAP.** Incoterms that set who pays for freight and insurance and where risk passes from seller to buyer. Explained properly in [Incoterms for spice importers](/blog/incoterms-for-spice-importers).

**Deposit.** Part-payment up front, often 30 to 50% for bulk orders.

**FCL / LCL.** A full container load versus sharing a container with other cargo.

**HS code.** The customs classification. Whole Ceylon cinnamon is 0906.11.

**L/C.** Letter of credit, a bank's promise to pay when compliant documents are presented.

**Lot.** A batch produced and tested together, with its own lot number.

**MOQ.** Minimum order quantity. Why suppliers set them is in [MOQs explained](/blog/moq-ceylon-cinnamon).

**NPQS.** Sri Lanka's National Plant Quarantine Service, which issues phytosanitary certificates.

**PGI.** Protected Geographical Indication. "Ceylon Cinnamon" is one in the EU.

**Private label.** Product packed under your brand instead of the supplier's.

**T/T.** Telegraphic transfer, a normal international bank wire.

## One more thing

If a term on one of our quotes or [spec sheets](/products) isn't on this list, or isn't clear in context, just ask. Most buying mistakes we see start with a misunderstood word, usually a grade or an Incoterm, and it's far cheaper to clear that up before an order than after.

**Samples are where buying decisions should be made: test the actual lot in your actual application before committing to volume.** A good sample request is specific, and a good evaluation is structured. Here is how to do both.

## How to request samples

Include:

- **Products and grades**, for example C5 Special and C5 quills, plus 60 mesh powder.
- **Quantity per sample**, typically 100 to 250 g.
- **Lot reference:** samples should come from the lot being offered.
- **Documents:** the lot's COA and spec sheet.
- **Destination country**, so the supplier can include the right paperwork for courier import.
- **Intended use and volume**, which helps the supplier suggest the right grade.

## Step-by-step evaluation

### 1. Check documents

Match the sample label's lot number to the COA. Check COA results against your specification. See [what a COA should include](/blog/certificate-of-analysis-cinnamon).

### 2. Visual inspection

- Colour and uniformity.
- Foxing, mould, insect damage.
- Foreign matter.
- For powder: colour, specks, lumps.

### 3. Measurement (quills)

- Diameter with calipers across 10 to 20 quills.
- Length and breakage.
- Quills per kilogram if you have enough sample.

See [quill diameter and length](/blog/cinnamon-quill-length-diameter).

### 4. Aroma and flavour

Run a simple sensory test: dry aroma, hot-water infusion, then taste in a neutral base. Score aroma, sweetness, heat, woodiness and off-notes. See [the flavour of Ceylon cinnamon](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-flavour-profile).

### 5. Application test

Use the sample in your real product: biscuit, beverage, blend or tea. Compare against your current cinnamon at equal and adjusted dosages.

### 6. Lab checks (optional but recommended)

For a new supplier or a critical product, send part of the sample to an accredited lab for moisture, volatile oil and coumarin, or a full panel for powder.

## Comparing suppliers fairly

- Evaluate **blind**, with coded samples.
- Use the **same tests** and **same people** for each sample.
- Compare **cost per unit of flavour**, not just price per kilo.

## Retain a reference sample

Keep part of the approved sample sealed, labelled and stored cool and dark. When the shipment arrives, compare it with the retained sample. This simple step resolves many disputes.

Nobody puts Hamburg-grade cinnamon in a glass jar on a deli counter. It's thick, it's patchy, and it isn't trying to impress anyone. But if your factory turns cinnamon into powder, extract or a beverage base, it might be the most sensible quill you can buy.

## What they are

The Hamburg family is the thickest of the four SLS 81 quill families. Compared with finer grades, Hamburg quills have:

- larger diameters and fewer quills per kilo;
- more foxing and colour variation allowed;
- the same species and the same Ceylon chemistry.

They're still whole quills of *Cinnamomum verum*, not chips. And note the numbering, which catches people out: within Hamburg, **H1 is the finest and H3 the coarsest**. In the C and M families it's the other way round. The full system is in [our grades guide](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide).

The name is a nod to history. Hamburg was one of Europe's great spice ports, and these coarse grades were traded there for processing.

## Flavour

Thick bark gives a fuller, woodier profile. Once it's ground or steeped, though, what comes through is recognisably Ceylon: sweet and warm, without cassia's sharpness. How strong it is depends on the lot's volatile oil, which is exactly why you check it.

## Where they make sense

- **Milling** into powder for food manufacturing and seasoning blends.
- **Extraction and distillation**, often alongside chips. See [cinnamon chips for extraction](/blog/cinnamon-chips-industrial-extraction).
- **Industrial infusion** for beverages and syrups.
- **Value food service packs.**

## Hamburg, C4 or chips?

| | C4 | Hamburg | Chips |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Form | Whole quill | Whole quill | Pieces |
| Looks | Fair | Coarse | Irregular |
| Easy to inspect? | Yes | Yes | Less so |
| Relative cost | Medium | Lower | Lowest |
| Best for | Food service, infusion | Milling, extraction | Milling, distillation, tea-cut |

The argument for Hamburg over chips is mostly about inspection. With whole quills, foreign matter and inconsistency are easy to spot. Some QA teams value that enough to pay the difference.

## What to specify

- Grade (H1, H2 or H3) and the SLS 81 edition.
- **Moisture** of 12% or less by ISO 939. Thick bark needs to be thoroughly dried, or mould follows.
- **Volatile oil**, with a minimum by ISO 6571. This is your flavour yield, and the single most important number for this grade.
- **Foreign matter** and **acid-insoluble ash** limits, which catch stems, sand and soil.
- Microbiology, if you won't be heat-treating after milling.

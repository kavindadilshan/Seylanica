**Cinnamon is a backbone spice in blends from garam masala and ras el hanout to pumpkin spice and mixed spice, and Ceylon cinnamon gives those blends a rounder, sweeter warmth than cassia.** For blenders, it is a lever for premium positioning and a more balanced flavour.

## Classic blends that use cinnamon

| Blend | Cuisine | Typical companions | What Ceylon brings |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Garam masala | South Asian | Cardamom, clove, cumin, coriander, pepper | Sweet, floral lift |
| Sri Lankan roasted curry powder | Sri Lankan | Coriander, cumin, fennel, curry leaf | Authentic origin. See [Sri Lankan cooking](/blog/sri-lankan-dishes-cinnamon) |
| Ras el hanout | North African | Cumin, ginger, coriander, rose, cardamom | Delicate warmth |
| Baharat | Middle Eastern | Black pepper, allspice, cumin, nutmeg | Rounded sweetness |
| Chinese five-spice | Chinese | Star anise, clove, fennel, Sichuan pepper | Softer than cassia |
| Pumpkin spice | American | Ginger, nutmeg, clove, allspice | Aromatic, less harsh |
| Mixed spice | British | Nutmeg, allspice, clove, coriander | Elegant bakery note |
| Mulling spices | European | Clove, orange peel, star anise | Sweet infusion |

## Formulation tips

- **Start with cinnamon as a supporting note** in savoury blends and as a lead note in sweet blends.
- **Balance strong spices:** clove and allspice can dominate; Ceylon's subtlety may need a slightly higher share.
- **Toast or not:** roasting whole spices before grinding deepens flavour, as in roasted curry powder.
- **Mesh matching:** use similar particle sizes to reduce segregation. See [powder mesh](/blog/cinnamon-powder-mesh-size).
- **Test in the dish**, not just by aroma.

## Quality and compliance in blends

- **Specify each ingredient**, including cinnamon's volatile oil and moisture. See [volatile oil](/blog/volatile-oil-cinnamon-iso-6571).
- **Microbiology:** blends for direct use need treated ingredients or a treatment step. See [microbiological testing](/blog/microbiology-testing-spices).
- **Mycotoxins:** spices such as nutmeg, ginger, turmeric, pepper and chilli have specific EU limits that can apply to blends containing them. See [mycotoxins](/blog/mycotoxins-aflatoxin-ochratoxin-spices).
- **Coumarin:** in sweet blends used at high doses in bakery, Ceylon helps with limits. See [coumarin](/blog/coumarin-in-cinnamon-explained).
- **Labelling:** blends need an ingredients list in descending order of weight. See [labelling](/blog/cinnamon-label-requirements-eu-us).
- **Customs:** mixtures are often classified differently from single spices. See [HS codes](/blog/hs-codes-cinnamon).

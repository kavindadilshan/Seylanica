**Mycotoxins are toxic compounds produced by certain moulds, and in spices the main concerns are aflatoxins and ochratoxin A.** Cinnamon bark is generally considered a lower-risk spice than chilli or nutmeg, but mould risk rises sharply with poor drying and damp storage, so many buyers still include mycotoxins in their testing plans.

## What mycotoxins are

Some moulds, particularly species of *Aspergillus* and *Penicillium*, produce toxins when they grow on crops under warm, humid conditions. The main mycotoxins in spices are:

- **Aflatoxins** (B1, B2, G1, G2), produced mainly by *Aspergillus flavus* and *A. parasiticus*.
- **Ochratoxin A (OTA)**, produced by some *Aspergillus* and *Penicillium* species.

They are stable and are not destroyed by normal cooking, which is why prevention matters.

## Which spices are most at risk

The EU sets specific aflatoxin limits for spices of the genera *Capsicum* (chilli, paprika), *Piper* (pepper), *Myristica* (nutmeg, mace), *Zingiber* (ginger) and *Curcuma* (turmeric), and mixtures containing them, under **Regulation (EU) 2023/915**. Ochratoxin A limits also apply to various spices. Check the current text for any limit relevant to your product.

## Where cinnamon fits

Cinnamon bark is generally regarded as lower risk, partly because of its composition and processing. But risk depends heavily on handling:

- Slow or incomplete drying.
- Storage in damp conditions.
- Condensation in shipping containers.
- Damaged packaging.

See [moisture in cinnamon](/blog/cinnamon-moisture-content-iso-939).

## Testing

- **Methods:** LC-MS/MS or HPLC with fluorescence detection after clean-up.
- **Sampling:** mycotoxins are unevenly distributed, so representative sampling matters even more than for other tests.
- **Frequency:** many buyers test a defined number of lots per year, or every lot for blends that include higher-risk spices.

## Prevention

- **Thorough shade drying** to specification moisture.
- **Dry, ventilated storage** off the floor.
- **Barrier packaging** and desiccants in containers.
- **Rejecting mouldy material** at intake and grading.
- **Rotating stock** to avoid long storage in humid conditions.

## In blends

If you blend cinnamon with higher-risk spices such as nutmeg or ginger in, for example, a pumpkin spice or garam masala, the finished blend may be subject to limits that apply to those ingredients. See [cinnamon in spice blends](/blog/cinnamon-spice-blends).

## Summary

Mycotoxins are a moisture problem first and a testing problem second. Control drying and storage, include mycotoxins in your risk-based testing plan, and pay special attention in blends.

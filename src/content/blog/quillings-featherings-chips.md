Every Ceylon quill leaves something behind. Pieces break during rolling and drying. Peelers trim edges and shave off thin layers. Some bark is too coarse or too thin to roll at all. None of it gets thrown away. It's sold as quillings, featherings and chips, and for many buyers it's the most sensible Ceylon cinnamon there is.

## Three different things

**Quillings** are broken pieces of quill. They come from rolling, drying, handling and grading, and they look like short, irregular fragments of the real thing.

**Featherings** are the thin inner shavings and small bits of bark that come off during peeling and trimming. Light, curly and very aromatic.

**Chips** are bark trimmings and bark that couldn't be peeled into quills. Coarser, chunkier, usually the cheapest form.

The trade descriptions follow long practice and SLS 81; the exact definitions and limits are in the standard itself, which we discuss in [SLS 81 explained](/blog/sls-81-standard-explained).

## Why buy them

They're the same species as the most expensive Alba quill. Same flavour character, same naturally low coumarin. If your cinnamon is going to be milled, steeped or distilled, you'll never see the difference between a perfect quill and a broken one, so there's little reason to pay for it.

## Where they go

**Powder.** Milling quillings and featherings is the standard, economical way to make ground Ceylon cinnamon. Mesh choices are covered in [cinnamon powder mesh size](/blog/cinnamon-powder-mesh-size).

**Tea.** Cut and sieved chips are the backbone of cinnamon teas and chai blends. More in [cinnamon in tea blending](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-tea-blending).

**Oil.** Chips are the usual raw material for bark oil distillation. See [chips for extraction](/blog/cinnamon-chips-industrial-extraction).

## The catch: variability

Quills are graded piece by piece. By-grades come in bulk, and quality can swing more from lot to lot. A tight specification fixes most of that. We'd include:

- **Size range.** For example 5 to 10 mm for chips, or 3 to 5 mm for tea-cut.
- **Dust.** Require dust extraction and a sieve analysis for anything going into tea bags.
- **Foreign matter and ash.** Stems, leaves, sand and soil (ISO 927, ISO 930). See [ash in cinnamon](/blog/cinnamon-ash-acid-insoluble-ash).
- **Volatile oil.** Your flavour yield. We specify 0.5% v/w or more for chips.
- **Moisture.** 12% or less.
- **Microbiology**, if the product will be used without a heat step.

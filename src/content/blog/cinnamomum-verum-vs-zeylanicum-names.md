It's common for a spec sheet to say *Cinnamomum verum* while the customs paperwork for the same shipment says *Cinnamomum zeylanicum*, and buyers are right to stop and ask whether they're getting the right thing. They are. It's the same plant with two names, and the confusion is common enough that it deserves a short explanation.

## Why one plant has two names

Scientific plant names follow international rules, and one of those rules is that the earliest validly published name usually wins. Over time, botanists have revised which name has priority for Ceylon cinnamon. Today *Cinnamomum verum* J.Presl is the name most references accept, and *Cinnamomum zeylanicum* Blume is treated as a synonym.

The names themselves are rather nice. *Zeylanicum* comes from Zeylan, one of the old names for Sri Lanka. *Verum* just means "true", as in true cinnamon.

## Where you'll see each one

| Where | Usually says |
| --- | --- |
| Modern botanical references | *C. verum* |
| Spec sheets and lab reports | Either, often both |
| HS tariff heading 0906.11 | *C. zeylanicum* Blume |
| Older trade and scientific literature | *C. zeylanicum* |
| Sri Lankan standards and industry papers | Both |

So it's perfectly normal for the customs side of a shipment to use one name and the quality side to use the other. We write *Cinnamomum verum* J.Presl (syn. *C. zeylanicum*) on our spec sheets so nobody has to wonder. If tariffs are your concern, see our note on [HS codes for cinnamon](/blog/hs-codes-cinnamon).

## The names that should make you look twice

The names worth watching for are the cassia species:

- *Cinnamomum cassia* (sometimes *C. aromaticum*), Chinese cassia
- *Cinnamomum burmannii*, Indonesian or Korintje cassia
- *Cinnamomum loureiroi*, Vietnamese or Saigon cassia

If one of these appears on a spec sheet for a product sold as Ceylon, something's wrong, whatever the marketing says. We compare them in [Saigon, Korintje and Chinese cassia](/blog/saigon-korintje-chinese-cassia-types).

## Why the botanical name is worth getting right

"Cinnamon" on its own is a loose word. The species name is precise, and that precision matters in a few practical places:

- **Specifications.** Your QA team can't verify "cinnamon". They can verify *C. verum* from Sri Lanka.
- **Compliance.** Coumarin levels differ hugely between species, which matters for foods with EU coumarin limits.
- **Customs.** 0906.11 is specific to *C. zeylanicum*. Other whole cinnamon falls under 0906.19, and anything ground under 0906.20.
- **Claims.** If you sell a product as Ceylon or true cinnamon, your documents should name the right species.

## What we'd write

On a purchase specification or lab report, we'd use this wording:

> *Cinnamomum verum* J.Presl (syn. *Cinnamomum zeylanicum* Blume), country of origin Sri Lanka.

**A certificate of origin proves where your cinnamon comes from, and preferential origin documents can also reduce or remove import duty.** For Sri Lankan spices, the main preferential schemes are the EU's Generalised Scheme of Preferences (GSP, where Sri Lanka is a GSP+ beneficiary) and the UK's Developing Countries Trading Scheme (DCTS).

## Two kinds of origin

| | Non-preferential origin | Preferential origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Customs, statistics, labelling, buyer requirements | Reduced or zero duty under a trade scheme |
| Typical document | Certificate of origin from an authorised body | Statement on origin (e.g. REX), or a preferential certificate |
| Rules | General origin rules | Scheme-specific rules of origin |

## Non-preferential certificate of origin

Issued in Sri Lanka by authorised bodies, it confirms that the goods originate in Sri Lanka. Buyers and authorities often require it, and it supports origin claims on labels and in marketing. See [the export documents checklist](/blog/export-documents-ceylon-cinnamon).

## EU: GSP and the REX system

Under the EU's GSP, exporters in beneficiary countries register in the **REX (Registered Exporter) system** and make a **statement on origin** on the commercial invoice or another commercial document. Below a value threshold, non-registered exporters can also make a statement. The importer uses the statement to claim the preference in the customs declaration.

For cinnamon, check the duty rate in TARIC for your exact code; where the standard rate is already zero, the preference has no duty effect, but origin evidence remains useful. See [importing into the EU](/blog/importing-cinnamon-into-eu).

## UK: Developing Countries Trading Scheme

The UK's **DCTS** provides tariff preferences for developing countries, including Sri Lanka. Origin is typically evidenced by a statement on origin under the scheme's rules. See [importing into the UK](/blog/importing-cinnamon-into-uk).

## Rules of origin for cinnamon

Cinnamon grown, harvested and processed in Sri Lanka is generally "wholly obtained" there, which keeps origin straightforward. Complications arise with:

- **Blends** containing spices from other countries.
- **Re-exports** or processing in third countries.
- **Packaging** done elsewhere.

## Origin and PGI

Origin documentation also supports use of the "Ceylon Cinnamon" protected geographical indication in the EU. See [Ceylon cinnamon's PGI status](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-pgi-geographical-indication).

## Practical tips

- Ask your supplier whether they are **REX-registered** if you import into the EU.
- Make sure the **statement wording** matches the scheme's required text.
- Keep the **HS code** consistent. See [HS codes for cinnamon](/blog/hs-codes-cinnamon).
- Keep origin documents for the **retention period** required by customs.

## Summary

Origin documents do two jobs: prove where your cinnamon is from and, where relevant, unlock preferential duty. Agree which documents you need at order stage.

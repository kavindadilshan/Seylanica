If we had to guess which question lands in our inbox most often, it would be some version of "C5 Special or C5?" They're the two grades that most of the export trade runs on, and the honest answer is that the choice depends far more on your packaging than on your recipe.

## How they compare

| | C5 Special | C5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typical diameter | About 8 to 10 mm | About 10 to 12 mm |
| Colour | Very even golden | Golden, with a little variation |
| Roll | Tight and uniform | Even |
| Flavour | Honeyed, light clove notes, smooth | Rounded, sweet and woody, clean |
| Volatile oil (our typical spec) | 1.0% v/w or more | 0.8% v/w or more |
| Our MOQ | 200 kg | 500 kg |
| Usually bought by | Gourmet retail, spice merchants | Importers, repackers, manufacturers |

These are our typical specifications; the figures that bind a shipment are on its lab report. The full sheets are here: [C5 Special](/products?spec=quills-c5sp) and [C5](/products?spec=quills-c5).

## When C5 Special earns its premium

C5 Special is the grade for people who sell cinnamon you can see. Clear jars and tubes in gourmet shops. Spice merchants who sell loose sticks by appearance. Cafés that put a stick on the saucer. Brands that want to sit above the mainstream without going all the way to Alba prices.

In all of those, a more even colour and tighter roll can move your retail price by more than they move your cost.

## When C5 is the smarter buy

If you're an importer supplying lots of different customers, a repacker cutting quills into mid-market sticks, or a manufacturer infusing or milling your own cinnamon, C5 makes more sense. The Ceylon character is the same; you're just not paying extra for a finish nobody will notice. It's also the grade we'd suggest for price-conscious retail lines where the origin matters more than perfect looks.

## Test it yourself

Don't take our word for it. Order samples of both and do three quick things:

1. Lay them side by side under good light and compare colour and roll.
2. Measure ten quills of each with calipers.
3. Steep a gram of each in hot water and taste them blind.

In our experience most teams find the flavour gap small and the visual gap obvious, which makes the decision easy once you know where your product will be seen. If you've never run a blind tasting, our [flavour guide](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-flavour-profile) has a simple method.

## A thought on margins

For cut sticks in a clear jar, the extra cost of C5 Special per jar is often small compared with the price difference shoppers will accept. For cinnamon hidden inside a product, C5 gives you the same flavour for less. We go further into the maths in [pricing Ceylon cinnamon for retail](/blog/retail-pricing-cinnamon).

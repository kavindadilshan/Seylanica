You don't need a laboratory to check whether you've received the grade you paid for. A pair of calipers, a ruler and a kitchen scale will tell you most of what you need to know, because Ceylon cinnamon grades are defined mainly by size.

## Why diameter matters most

Under SLS 81, quill grades are set primarily by maximum diameter. Thinner quills are harder to roll, contain more fine layers, look more refined, and are scarcer, so they cost more. A shipment sold as C5 that turns out to be mostly C4-sized quills is a real quality problem, and a measurable one.

For reference, our typical diameters are 6 mm or less for Alba, about 8 to 10 mm for C5 Special, about 10 to 12 mm for C5 and about 13 to 14 mm for C4. The full system is in [our grades guide](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide).

## Why length matters too

The standard quill is about 42 cm. That length suits cartons and cutting, and it's what buyers expect. Short pieces and broken bits are sold separately as quillings, at a lower price, so a "quill" shipment with lots of breakage is effectively a partial downgrade. For retail sticks, cut length becomes the key spec, which we cover in [choosing a cut length](/blog/cut-cinnamon-sticks-sizes).

## A simple check on arrival

1. **Sample from several cartons**, not just the top layer of the first one you open.
2. **Measure diameter** at the middle of each quill with calipers. If you want to be thorough, measure both ends too.
3. **Measure length** with a ruler.
4. **Count quills per kilogram** on a weighed sample. It's a quick proxy for thickness and easy to compare between lots.
5. **Weigh the broken pieces** in your sample and work out the percentage.
6. **Compare** everything with the specification and the supplier's own pre-shipment data.

Record the results. Over a few shipments, you'll build a clear picture of how consistent a supplier is.

## A spec block for quills

| Parameter | Example for C5 |
| --- | --- |
| Grade | C5, SLS 81:2021 |
| Diameter | About 10 to 12 mm, with an agreed tolerance |
| Length | 42 cm |
| Broken pieces | Agreed maximum % by weight |
| Moisture | 12% m/m max (ISO 939) |
| Volatile oil | 0.8% v/w min, typical (ISO 6571) |

## Size isn't flavour

Thinner quills tend to taste more delicate and thicker ones fuller and woodier, but diameter doesn't tell you how aromatic a lot is. Volatile oil does. Use both together: diameter to confirm the grade, volatile oil to confirm the flavour you're paying for. There's more on that in [volatile oil explained](/blog/volatile-oil-cinnamon-iso-6571).

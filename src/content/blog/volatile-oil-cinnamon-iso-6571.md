Cinnamon is bought for its smell and taste, but those are hard things to put in a contract. Volatile oil content gets you closest. It measures how much of the aromatic essential oil is actually in the bark, and it's one of the few lab numbers that maps fairly directly onto what you experience when you open a bag.

## What's being measured

Ceylon cinnamon's aroma comes from a mix of volatile compounds in the bark. Cinnamaldehyde is the most prominent, alongside eugenol, linalool and others. Together they make up the bark's essential oil. As a general rule, more oil means a fuller, stronger aroma.

## How ISO 6571 works

The lab grinds a weighed sample, puts it in a distillation apparatus with water and steam-distils it for a set time. The oil collects in a graduated tube, and the volume is read off. The result is expressed as millilitres of oil per 100 g of sample, written as % v/w. Check whether a figure is on a dry basis or as received; it makes a difference when comparing reports.

These are our typical specifications. The binding numbers for a shipment are on its COA.

## Using it to buy smarter

**Comparing lots of the same grade.** Two lots of C5 can look identical and still differ in aroma. Volatile oil tells you which one gives you more per kilo.

**Comparing value across grades.** If you're grinding or infusing, you don't care about looks; you care about aroma per dollar. A thicker grade with good oil can beat a finer grade comfortably on that measure. That's the case we make for [C4](/blog/c4-cinnamon-grade-uses) and for [by-grades](/blog/quillings-featherings-chips).

**Keeping product consistent.** A minimum in your spec stops weak lots slipping through.

**Spotting trends.** Over a year of COAs, you'll see how a supplier's lots vary by season.

## Protecting it

The word "volatile" is a clue. The oil escapes:

- **in milling**, if grinders run hot (see [powder mesh size](/blog/cinnamon-powder-mesh-size));
- **in some heat treatments**, though well-run steam sterilisation keeps losses small ([steam vs irradiation](/blog/steam-sterilisation-vs-irradiation-spices));
- **in storage**, with heat, light, air and time ([shelf life and storage](/blog/cinnamon-shelf-life-storage)).

If you add only one quality number beyond moisture to your cinnamon spec, we'd make it this one. Every [spec in our catalog](/products) lists volatile oil with its ISO method, so you've got something solid to compare against.

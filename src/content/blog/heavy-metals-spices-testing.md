Heavy metals have become a bigger topic in the spice world over the last few years. Regulators have tightened limits, retailers have added their own, and several high-profile cases in other spices have made buyers more careful. For cinnamon importers, the practical answer is simple: test every lot, and know which limits apply where you sell.

## Where metals come from

There are a handful of routes:

- **Soil and geology.** Natural mineral content and past land use.
- **Water** used for growing and processing.
- **Air.** Industrial and traffic emissions settle on crops.
- **Processing.** Worn grinders and unsuitable surfaces can contribute.
- **Fraud.** In rare cases in the wider spice trade, colourants containing metals have been added to improve appearance. It's one reason buyers are wary of unusually bright powders. More on that in [cinnamon adulteration](/blog/cinnamon-adulteration-detection).

## The rules

**EU.** Regulation (EU) 2023/915 replaced the older Regulation (EC) No 1881/2006 and sets maximum levels for contaminants in food, including lead and cadmium limits for certain spice categories. Check the current consolidated text for bark spices like cinnamon.

**UK.** Great Britain has its own contaminant rules, based on retained law and amended since.

**US.** The FDA monitors toxic elements in foods and has set action levels and guidance for some categories. Many US buyers apply internal limits on top.

Because all of this changes, we'd always write contracts to "the limits in force at the date of shipment" rather than copying today's numbers into a document you'll still be using in three years.

## How it's tested

The standard method is **ICP-MS** (inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry) after microwave digestion of the sample. Mercury sometimes gets its own method. The lab should be ISO/IEC 17025 accredited with spices in scope, and should report each element in mg/kg with its limit of quantification.

## In your specification

| Element | What to specify |
| --- | --- |
| Lead (Pb) | Destination limit for the spice category, or your internal limit |
| Cadmium (Cd) | Same |
| Mercury (Hg) | Same |
| Arsenic (As) | Same; state total or inorganic as appropriate |

We'd ask for results on every powder lot and at a defined frequency for quills.

## Lowering the risk

Source from known growing areas with documented traceability (see [garden to port](/blog/from-garden-to-port-traceability)). Make sure processing uses clean, food-grade equipment. Be suspicious of colour that seems too good. And send the occasional lot to your own lab.

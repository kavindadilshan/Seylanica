Picking a cut length sounds trivial until you've ordered sticks that don't quite fit your jar. Then it becomes very important, very fast. Here's how we'd think it through.

## The usual options

| Length | Works well for | Fits | Worth knowing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 cm | Drink stirrers, gift sets, small tubes | Small jars, sachets | Great in hot drinks and cocktails |
| 8 cm | Everyday retail | Standard spice jars | A safe middle choice |
| 10 cm | Food service, mulled drinks, larger packs | Tall jars, pouches | Looks generous, fewer sticks per pack |
| 42 cm (whole) | Importers, repackers, display | Cartons | Cheapest per kilo; cut it yourself |

We supply quills at 42 cm or cut to 5, 8 or 10 cm. The grades are in our [catalog](/products).

## Which grade to cut

Because every stick is on show, grade matters more for cut sticks than for anything else.

- **Alba** makes the most elegant sticks. Ideal for luxury tubes. See [Alba cinnamon](/blog/alba-cinnamon-grade).
- **C5 Special** is the premium retail workhorse.
- **C5** works well in mid-market jars and pouches. Our comparison is in [C5 Special vs C5](/blog/c5-special-vs-c5-cinnamon).
- **C4** is fine for food service and value packs, but looks chunky next to the finer grades.

## What to write in your spec

- **Length with a tolerance**, for example 8 cm ± 0.5 cm.
- **The maximum share of broken or short pieces**, by weight. Some is unavoidable; agree how much.
- **Clean cut ends** without much splitting.
- **Grade and diameter range.**
- **Moisture** of 12% or less, because sealed retail packs trap any dampness.
- **Packing** that stops the sticks being crushed in transit.

## Cut at origin, or cut yourself?

If you buy cut at origin, you get consistent sizes ready to pack, at a slightly higher price per kilo. If you buy whole quills and cut them locally, you save on the cinnamon but need equipment, labour, and a plan for all the short pieces that come off. For most brands starting out, buying cut at origin is simpler. We go into the wider decision in our [private label guide](/blog/private-label-ceylon-cinnamon-guide).

## Test in your packaging first

This is the step people skip. Before you order a few hundred kilos, put sample sticks in your actual packs:

- Do they slide into the tube without forcing?
- How many fit in the jar, and does that match your declared net weight?
- Do the ends poke through a pouch film?

It takes an afternoon and saves a lot of grief. Our article on [packaging options](/blog/cinnamon-packaging-options) has more on matching pack to product. When you're ready, [ask for samples in two lengths](/quote?type=sample) and try them for real.

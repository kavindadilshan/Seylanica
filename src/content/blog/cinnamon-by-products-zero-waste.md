**Making Ceylon quills produces a stream of by-products: broken quills, inner shavings, bark chips, leaves and stripped wood.** The industry turns almost all of it into something useful: powder, tea-cut, bark oil, leaf oil and more. That efficiency matters for both sustainability and cost.

## What is produced, and where it goes

| By-product | Source | Main uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quillings | Broken quills | Powder, value retail |
| Featherings | Inner shavings and small pieces | Powder, tea blending, distillation |
| Chips | Bark trimmings, unpeelable bark | Bark oil, powder, tea-cut |
| Leaves and twigs | Collected at harvest | Leaf oil (eugenol-rich) |
| Stripped wood | Stems after peeling | Local fuel and other uses |

See [quillings, featherings and chips](/blog/quillings-featherings-chips) and [bark oil vs leaf oil](/blog/cinnamon-bark-oil-vs-leaf-oil).

## Why it matters

- **Farmer income:** selling by-products adds value per hectare.
- **Lower cost** for buyers who do not need whole quills.
- **Less waste** from each harvest.
- **Resource efficiency:** more product from the same land and labour.

## Choosing by-grades as a buyer

If your cinnamon will be ground, steeped or distilled, by-grades can be the smarter and more sustainable choice:

- **Powder production:** quillings and featherings. See [powder mesh](/blog/cinnamon-powder-mesh-size).
- **Tea blending:** tea-cut chips. See [tea blending](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-tea-blending).
- **Extraction and distillation:** chips. See [chips for extraction](/blog/cinnamon-chips-industrial-extraction).

## Quality controls for by-products

By-products vary more than quills, so specify:

- Size range and dust levels.
- Foreign matter and acid-insoluble ash. See [ash](/blog/cinnamon-ash-acid-insoluble-ash).
- Moisture. See [moisture](/blog/cinnamon-moisture-content-iso-939).
- Volatile oil. See [volatile oil](/blog/volatile-oil-cinnamon-iso-6571).
- Microbiology for direct-use powder.

## Telling the story

"Made from the whole harvest" can be a meaningful message when backed by evidence. Keep sustainability claims specific and accurate. See [sustainable sourcing](/blog/sustainable-cinnamon-sourcing).

## Summary

Ceylon cinnamon processing is close to zero-waste: bark by-products become powder and oil, leaves become leaf oil, and wood is reused.

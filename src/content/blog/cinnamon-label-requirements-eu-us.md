**A compliant cinnamon label shows the product name, net quantity, date marking, the responsible business, and a lot code, plus market-specific details.** The EU and UK follow food information rules based on Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011; the US follows FDA rules. Here is a practical overview. Always check the current rules or take advice for your products.

## Core elements by market

| Element | EU | UK (GB) | US |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Name of the food | Required | Required | Statement of identity required |
| Net quantity | Metric, required | Metric, required | US customary and metric |
| Date marking | Best before | Best before | Not federally required for spices; commonly used |
| Business name and address | EU business | UK business | Manufacturer, packer or distributor |
| Lot code | Required | Required | Strongly recommended |
| Ingredients list | Not always for single ingredients | Same | Not required for single-ingredient foods named by their ingredient |
| Allergens | If present | If present | If present |
| Nutrition declaration | Spices generally exempt | Same | Spices with insignificant nutrients generally exempt |
| Country of origin | Required in certain cases | Required in certain cases | Generally required for imports |
| Storage instructions | Where needed | Where needed | Optional |
| Language | Official language(s) of sale country | English | English |

## Naming the product

Be specific and accurate:

- "Ceylon Cinnamon Sticks" or "True Cinnamon Sticks (*Cinnamomum verum*)".
- "Ground Ceylon Cinnamon".

Use "Ceylon" only for genuine Sri Lankan *C. verum*, and use the EU PGI symbol only where entitled. See [PGI status](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-pgi-geographical-indication).

## Date marking

Set best-before dates based on shelf-life evidence. See [shelf life and storage](/blog/cinnamon-shelf-life-storage).

## Lot coding

A lot code links each pack to its export lot and COA, enabling targeted recalls. See [garden-to-port traceability](/blog/from-garden-to-port-traceability).

## Allergens

Cinnamon is not one of the major listed allergens in the EU or US. However:

- **Sulphites** above 10 mg/kg (as SO2) must be declared. See [sulphur dioxide in cinnamon](/blog/sulphur-dioxide-cinnamon).
- **Cross-contact** with allergens in shared facilities may need precautionary labelling based on risk assessment.

## Claims

- **Avoid health claims** unless authorised for your market. See [marketing spices without health claims](/blog/compliant-marketing-claims-spices).
- Only use **organic** claims and logos if certified. See [organic Ceylon cinnamon](/blog/organic-ceylon-cinnamon).
- Treatment: irradiated spices must be labelled as such.

## Practical checklist

- [ ] Product name accurate and specific.
- [ ] Net quantity in correct units and font size.
- [ ] Best-before with storage instructions.
- [ ] Business name and address for the market.
- [ ] Country of origin.
- [ ] Lot code.
- [ ] Allergens and sulphites assessed.
- [ ] Claims reviewed.
- [ ] Language requirements met.

## Summary

Get the mandatory elements right first, then design around them. Review claims carefully and tie every origin or certification statement to documents.

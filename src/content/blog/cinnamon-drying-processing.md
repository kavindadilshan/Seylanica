**After peeling, Ceylon cinnamon quills are dried slowly in the shade, then graded, sorted, cut or milled, and packed.** Each step affects colour, aroma, moisture and food safety. Done well, processing preserves everything the peeler created.

## Step 1: Shade drying

Freshly rolled quills are laid on racks, often strung on coir rope or placed on raised frames, in a shaded, ventilated space. Over several days they lose moisture, curl tighter and turn golden.

**Why shade:** slow, even drying keeps colour attractive, reduces cracking and limits loss of volatile oils.

## Step 2: Further drying and conditioning

Quills may move to drying rooms or covered areas to reach target moisture. Moisture is checked before packing. See [moisture in cinnamon](/blog/cinnamon-moisture-content-iso-939).

## Step 3: Grading and sorting

Quills are sorted by diameter, colour and quality into SLS 81 grades. Broken pieces become quillings. See [grades explained](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide).

## Step 4: Cutting

For retail and food service, quills are cut to 5, 8 or 10 cm. See [cut sticks](/blog/cut-cinnamon-sticks-sizes).

## Step 5: Milling (powder)

Quillings, featherings or quills are milled and sieved to the target mesh, with temperature control to protect aroma. Metal detection or magnets remove foreign metal. See [powder mesh](/blog/cinnamon-powder-mesh-size).

## Step 6: Treatment (optional)

Powder may be steam-sterilised to reduce microbial load. See [steam sterilisation](/blog/steam-sterilisation-vs-irradiation-spices).

## Step 7: Testing and release

Lots are tested and released only when results are in specification. See [what a COA should include](/blog/certificate-of-analysis-cinnamon).

## Step 8: Packing

Quills go into lined cartons or vacuum bags; powder into multi-wall bags or pouches. Lot numbers are printed on every unit. See [packaging options](/blog/cinnamon-packaging-options).

## Hygiene throughout

| Practice | Protects against |
| --- | --- |
| Raised drying racks | Soil contamination, high ash |
| Covered drying areas | Rain, birds, animals |
| Clean, dry storage | Mould, insects |
| Worker hygiene | Microbial contamination |
| Pest control | Infestation |
| No unnecessary fumigation | Residues. See [sulphur dioxide](/blog/sulphur-dioxide-cinnamon) |

## Summary

Drying and processing are where good bark becomes a good product, or a problem. Slow shade drying, careful grading, controlled milling and hygienic handling protect aroma and safety. See our [Quality & Origin page](/quality) for the controls behind every Seylanica lot.

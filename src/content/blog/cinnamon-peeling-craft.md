**Every Ceylon cinnamon quill is made by hand, by a skilled peeler who scrapes, rubs, peels and rolls the inner bark into a long, layered roll.** This craft is what makes Ceylon cinnamon look, feel and taste the way it does, and it is the main reason it costs more than cassia.

## Step by step

### 1. Harvest

Shoots are cut from coppiced bushes, usually in the morning and soon after rain, when sap is flowing and the bark lifts easily. See [cultivation and coppicing](/blog/cinnamon-cultivation-coppicing).

### 2. Preparing the stem

Leaves and side branches are removed. The stems are kept moist and processed quickly.

### 3. Scraping

The peeler scrapes away the thin outer bark with a curved knife, revealing the pale, aromatic inner bark.

### 4. Rubbing

The stem is rubbed firmly with a **brass rod**, loosening the inner bark from the wood without tearing it.

### 5. Peeling

The peeler makes cuts around and along the stem and carefully lifts the inner bark off in long, thin strips.

### 6. Rolling (making the quill)

Strips are laid end to end and overlapped, with smaller pieces packed inside as filler, and rolled together into a long quill, traditionally about 42 cm. The thinner and more even the strips, the finer the resulting grade.

### 7. Drying

Quills dry slowly in the shade, then move to drying areas, curling tighter and turning golden. See [drying and processing](/blog/cinnamon-drying-processing).

## Why the skill matters

| Peeler skill affects | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Strip thinness | Finer grades like Alba |
| Even overlap | Tight, uniform quills |
| Gentle handling | Fewer breaks, less waste |
| Clean scraping | Better colour, less foxing |
| Speed | Freshness and cost |

See [Ceylon cinnamon grades explained](/blog/ceylon-cinnamon-grades-guide).

## By-products

The process produces featherings, quillings and chips, which are sold for grinding and distillation, while leaves can be distilled for leaf oil. See [by-products and zero-waste processing](/blog/cinnamon-by-products-zero-waste).

## The future of the craft

Fewer young people are entering peeling, and labour costs are rising. Training programmes, better pay, safer tools and partial mechanisation are all part of the response. See [the skills gap in cinnamon peeling](/blog/cinnamon-skills-training-peelers).

Ceylon cinnamon is a handmade product. The peeler's skill shapes its grade, its look and its price. When you buy Alba or C5 Special, you are buying that craft. Explore our [quill grades](/products) or read about [sustainable sourcing](/blog/sustainable-cinnamon-sourcing).

**Clean label, the demand for simple, recognisable, transparently sourced ingredients, is reshaping how spices are bought and sold.** For cinnamon, it favours a named species, a named origin, no unnecessary additives and processing that consumers understand. Ceylon cinnamon fits that brief well.

## What clean label means for spices

| Expectation | Example for cinnamon |
| --- | --- |
| Named ingredient | "Ceylon cinnamon (*Cinnamomum verum*)" |
| Named origin | "From Sri Lanka" |
| No unnecessary additives | No anti-caking agents or colours |
| Understandable processing | Steam-treated rather than irradiated |
| Transparency | Lot traceability, open information |

## Why it is growing

- **Consumer curiosity** about where food comes from.
- **Retailer standards** and own-label requirements.
- **Food safety incidents** in spices raising awareness.
- **Social media** spreading information about cassia vs Ceylon.

## Traceability makes it real

Clean-label claims must be backed by facts. Lot-level traceability, COAs and origin documents allow brands to answer questions confidently. See [garden-to-port traceability](/blog/from-garden-to-port-traceability).

## Processing choices

- **Steam sterilisation** is widely accepted in clean-label ranges. See [steam vs irradiation vs EtO](/blog/steam-sterilisation-vs-irradiation-spices).
- **Irradiated** spices must be labelled, which most clean-label brands avoid.
- **Sulphur fumigation** can leave residues; specify limits. See [sulphur dioxide](/blog/sulphur-dioxide-cinnamon).

## How suppliers can respond

1. Provide **complete specifications** and COAs.
2. Offer **untreated and steam-treated** options.
3. Supply **origin information** that brands can use.
4. Maintain **lot traceability** to retail packs.

## How brands can respond

1. Use **specific names**: "Ceylon cinnamon", not just "cinnamon".
2. State **origin** where it is accurate and documented.
3. Keep **ingredient lists short**.
4. Avoid **vague claims** like "pure" or "natural" without context.
5. Never drift into **health claims**. See [marketing without health claims](/blog/compliant-marketing-claims-spices).

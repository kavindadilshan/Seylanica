It's easy to over-buy on grade. New buyers often start with finer quills than their product needs, then realise most of that cinnamon is going into a pot, a kettle or a grinder where nobody sees it. That's usually the point where C4 starts making sense. Not because quality matters less, but because the money is better spent elsewhere.

## What C4 is

C4 is a continental grade under SLS 81, thicker than C5 and with a little more tolerance for surface variation. Our typical spec looks like this:

| | C4 (typical) |
| --- | --- |
| Quill diameter | About 13 to 14 mm |
| Length | 42 cm, or cut to size |
| Moisture | 12% m/m or less (ISO 939) |
| Volatile oil | 0.7% v/w or more (ISO 6571) |
| Foreign matter | 1% m/m or less (ISO 927) |
| MOQ | 500 kg |

The binding numbers for a shipment are always on its lab report. The full [C4 spec sheet](/products?spec=quills-c4) is in our catalog.

## What it tastes like

Fuller and woodier than the finer grades, with clear Ceylon sweetness and none of cassia's burn. In anything cooked for a while, like a curry, a braise or a pot of mulled wine, that extra body is a plus rather than a compromise.

## Where C4 does its best work

**Food service kitchens.** Curries, rice dishes, braises, syrups and mulled drinks. Nobody sees the stick, but everyone tastes it.

**Infusions.** Teas, cordials and syrups where the cinnamon is strained out. Our [infusions guide](/blog/infusing-cinnamon-syrups-spirits) covers technique.

**In-house milling.** Manufacturers who grind their own powder get whole-quill traceability at a sensible price.

**Value retail.** 1 kg trade packs for caterers and ethnic grocers.

**Savoury cooking** generally. There's more on that in [cinnamon beyond dessert](/blog/cinnamon-in-savoury-cooking).

## Where you'd want something finer

If the stick is on display in a premium jar, C4 will look chunky next to [C5 Special or Alba](/blog/c5-special-vs-c5-cinnamon). And in very light, delicate dishes, the floral lift of the finer grades is noticeable.

## C4 or chips for grinding?

If your cinnamon is headed for a grinder, C4 isn't the only option. Chips and quillings are cheaper per kilo. The trade-off is that whole quills are much easier to inspect: you can see foreign matter, judge colour and check consistency at a glance. Chips vary more and usually need more cleaning. Plenty of manufacturers stick with C4 for that reason alone. We compare them properly in [quillings, featherings and chips](/blog/quillings-featherings-chips).

## What to watch when you buy C4

Ask for **volatile oil** on every lab report. With value grades, it's your best measure of how much aroma you're actually getting per kilo. Check **moisture** too, because thicker quills can hold water if drying was hurried. For bigger orders, ask about **pressed bales**, which pack more weight into a container.
